About Shape Chicago

Last updated November 12, 2026

Shape Chicago is a free, independent site about how Chicago builds homes, runs transit and spends its money. It counts what actually happens, explains why, and lets you test a change before City Hall votes on it.

Who makes it

It's built by a Chicago development and housing professional, with AI coding tools doing the programming. It started as a side project to answer one question: why doesn't Chicago build more homes? It grew from there.

It's independent and fan-made: not affiliated with the City of Chicago, the CTA, Metra, Pace, any alderperson, campaign or party, or the builder's employer.

What's here

  • Simulators for housing, transit, the city budget, pensions and the Bears stadium. They're estimates, not forecasts, and each one lists every assumption it makes.
  • What can I build on my lot? Type an address to see what the zoning allows there.
  • Grants finder and Affordable deal check: the housing programs you might qualify for, and a first look at how an affordable building on a site would pencil.
  • Progress: homes permitted, zoning, rents, transit, streets and city money, counted from public records and updated every day.
  • Your ward: how your alderperson voted on housing, every rezoning in the ward and the next chance to weigh in.
  • News and Learn: the day's planning stories from local newsrooms, and short explainers.

How the numbers are handled

  • Every number links to its source or is labeled as an estimate, with the assumption written out.
  • Data comes from public records: the City of Chicago, Cook County, the State of Illinois, the CTA, Metra and federal agencies. Each page shows the date its data runs through.
  • Counts are checked against official totals. For example, our permit count matches the Census Bureau's survey within about 1% over 2010–2025.
  • Records about elected officials stick to what they did (votes, ordinances they introduced). The site takes no side in elections.

What we won't do

  • Show private people's names or personal details, on the site or in the code.
  • Ask you to make an account, or use ad networks or tracking cookies (Privacy lists exactly what we receive).
  • Let anyone pay to change what a page or simulator says.

Corrections

Spotted something wrong? Send a note with the page and what's off. We fix errors and list the ones that changed a number here.
