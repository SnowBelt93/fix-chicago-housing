The new transit law’s deadlines

In 2025 the state replaced the regional transit agency with a stronger one (NITA) and gave it new money. These are dates the law sets.

  1. June 1, 2026The law takes effect. The regional board approves a quarter-point sales tax increase
  2. Sept. 1, 2026New boards are seated at the regional agency (NITA), CTA, Metra and Pace
  3. Dec. 31, 2026The first regional budget under the new law must pass
  4. July 1, 2027Transit ambassadors start work on the system
  5. Dec. 31, 2027Service standards for buses and trains adopted
  6. Mid-2028Fare capping and income-based fares in place
  7. Feb. 1, 2030One fare system across CTA, Metra and Pace

Dates are from the agency’s summary of the law (June 2026). A passed date means the deadline has gone by; we don’t yet track whether each was met. What the law changed ›

The official goal

The 2025 transit law

There is no official ridership target. What the law does set is the list of deadlines above, plus a rule that fares and other income cover 25% of running costs from 2028 and 20% from 2029. The agency’s summary ›

In 2025 CTA changed how it counts riders: new fareboxes and passenger counters, and rides the old system missed. That added 5.9% to its 2025 total (8.5% on buses, 2.4% on the L). On the new count 2025 was 338.0 million rides; on the old count, 319.2 million, which is the figure compared with 2019 here. The 2026 gain compares two years on the new count.
