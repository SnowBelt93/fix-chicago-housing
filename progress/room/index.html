Room to grow

Today’s zoning allows about 110,000 more homes on empty private lots outside downtown. Nearly everything else it allows would mean replacing a building.

Zoning map and Assessor lots, 2026 · checked Nov. 9, 2026

This counts homes per lot only. Height limits, parking rules and what is worth building all cut the real number, and at the pace lots are actually built on, most of this room stays unused.

On paper, zoning outside downtown allows about 484,000 more homes

Where they would go:

Empty lots · 110,000Lots with a shop or other building, no homes · 171,000Lots that already have homes · 203,000

Only the first group could be built without replacing or adding to a building that stands there now.

Ward 42 shows no empty-lot room because nearly all of it is downtown zoning, which is counted apart.

Downtown is counted apart, and roughly

Downtown zoning limits floor area, not homes per lot. Unused floor area there works out to somewhere between 46,000 and 80,000 more homes at 900 sq ft each. Building heights and lot sizes are missing for hundreds of downtown lots, so this is a rough range and those lots are left out.

No official target

No city program sets a target for building on empty lots

This section is context for the others: it shows how much could be built without changing any zoning.

For each lot: the homes its zoning district allows (lot size ÷ the district’s minimum lot area per home) minus the homes there now. Lot sizes are the Cook County Assessor’s; 51,854 of the 110,000 are on lots with a measured size and the rest assume a standard 3,125 sq ft lot. City-owned and other tax-exempt land, railroads, planned developments, manufacturing districts and parks are left out. Two-flats that an exception allows in some single-family districts are counted as over.
