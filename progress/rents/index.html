Rents and prices are the result the other sections add up to, so this section is a readout, not a scorecard.

ZIP code rents are HUD’s Small Area Fair Market Rents for fiscal 2027 and 2019: for each ZIP code, the rent plus utilities that 40% of recently rented standard homes come in under. HUD builds them from Census surveys a few years old plus a trend, so they trail the market; no ZIP code can be set above 150% of the metro area’s figure or below the state’s minimum (HUD, fiscal 2027 Small Area Fair Market Rent methodology). ZIP code price changes are the Federal Housing Finance Agency’s yearly house price index, which follows repeat sales and refinancings of the same homes and is published only where there are enough of them. Asking rent is Zillow’s Observed Rent Index for the city: rents advertised for homes on the market, all sizes, smoothed. It runs above what sitting tenants pay. The 2026-dollar figures use the national Consumer Price Index (yearly averages; 2026 is the average of the months published so far, so the last step is an estimate). Sale prices are the median of what sold that year, not the same homes. They come from the Cook County Assessor’s sales in Chicago, leaving out sales under $10,000, resales within a year, unusual deeds and multi-parcel sales.
