Progress › Decisions

The City Council has until the end of the year to pass a 2027 budget that closes an $882 million gap.

Deadline: · checked Nov. 11, 2026

$882Mgap between what the city expects to collect and to spend in 2027
26 votesneeded in the City Council to pass a budget
28alderpeople signed a letter on Nov. 10 calling the Mayor’s plan a “non-starter”

Who decides

The Mayor proposes the budget and the City Council passes it. It takes 26 of the 50 alderpeople’s votes.

The Mayor’s plan

  • Bring back a head tax on big employers: $25 a month per worker at companies with 125 or more full-time workers, about $101 million a year
  • Raise the tax on leased equipment and cloud software from 15% to 20%, about $234 million a year
  • Put $75 million from the parking meter deal toward 2027 pension payments
  • Savings, one-time money and other fees for the rest (each one is in the budget simulator)

What the 28 alderpeople wrote

  • No more than $300 million in new or higher taxes
  • No head tax and no lease tax increase (the lease tax went from 11% to 15% in the 2026 budget)
  • Collect more of the money people and businesses already owe the city
  • The Council voted 44–6 to spend the $75 million on an extra pension payment this year instead

The dates

  1. OctoberThe Mayor sends his 2027 budget to the City Council
  2. Nov. 10Department hearings end; 28 alderpeople send their letter
  3. Dec. 8–9Next full City Council meetings (a final vote may come later)
  4. Dec. 31Deadline to pass a 2027 budget. Last year’s passed on Dec. 20
